## Research Overview

The Structural Artificial Intelligence Laboratory studies how structures behave under extreme loading, and how machine learning can make that behaviour predictable at a cost that engineering practice can actually bear. Full-scale testing tells us what real structures do; physics-informed models and high-fidelity simulation tell us why, and let those findings generalise well beyond the specimens we can afford to build.

## Research Directions

### Composite and Modular Structures

High-performance steel-concrete composite structures, modular structures and their connection joints, structural performance and design optimisation.

Modular integrated construction (MiC) moves building work off site, but leaves open how semi-rigid inter-module connections govern system behaviour under seismic loading. We combine full-scale experiments with high-fidelity simulation to develop design methods for composite shear walls, high-resilience joints and volumetric modular buildings — including systems using advanced high-strength steel and ultra-high-performance concrete. Underpinning this work are three-dimensional constitutive models for concrete and steel, and the nonlinear finite element formulations needed to simulate composite members with quantified accuracy.

### Structural Digital Twins and Intelligent Design

Combining finite element analysis, machine learning and engineering data to carry out structural condition identification, performance prediction, generative design and intelligent optimisation. Developing digital twin models of real, complex structures under extreme loading. Developing scientific AI algorithms that solve for the spatio-temporal response of complex engineering structures.

Purely data-driven networks extrapolate badly, which is fatal in structural engineering, where the loading cases that matter most are exactly the ones outside the training data. We embed governing equations and mechanics constraints directly into the learning problem, so predictions stay physically admissible where data is thin. Neural operators learn the solution map of the underlying PDEs, turning analyses that take hours into surrogates that evaluate in milliseconds — fast enough for a design optimisation loop or a live digital twin, and accurate enough to base engineering decisions on.

### Physical AI and Embodied Intelligent Robotics

Researching AI methods that fuse physical mechanisms with data-driven learning, together with multimodal perception, environment understanding, motion planning, autonomous decision-making and reinforcement learning algorithms for robots; and exploring the application of embodied intelligent robots in complex scenarios such as construction, fabrication, inspection and maintenance.

These are settings where a robot has to reason about structures, not merely navigate around them — which is where the physical-AI half of the group's work meets the structural half.
