## Welcome

Welcome to the **Structural Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL)** at The University of Hong Kong, led by **Prof. Jiaji Wang**, Assistant Professor in the Department of Civil Engineering.

Our group works at the meeting point of **computational mechanics** and **machine learning**. We build physics-informed models, neural operators and high-fidelity finite element formulations that predict how structures behave — from the constitutive response of concrete at the material scale, through steel-concrete composite members, up to complete modular buildings under earthquake loading. The same models feed digital twins and structural health monitoring systems that keep watch over structures once they are built.

Research in the group is deliberately two-sided. Large-scale experiments in the laboratory tell us what real structures actually do; simulation and learning tell us why, and let us extend those findings far beyond the specimens we can afford to test. Neither half is much use without the other.

## Research Themes

- **Physics-informed machine learning** — embedding governing equations and mechanics constraints directly into neural networks so that predictions stay physically admissible outside the training data
- **Neural operators for mechanics** — learning solution operators for PDEs governing solids and structures, giving near-real-time surrogates for expensive nonlinear analyses
- **Constitutive modelling and nonlinear FEM** — three-dimensional concrete and steel models for high-fidelity simulation of composite structures
- **Steel-concrete composite and modular structures** — experiments and design methods for composite shear walls, joints and volumetric modular buildings
- **Digital twins and structural health monitoring** — data-driven condition assessment of bridges and buildings in service
- **Seismic isolation and resilience** — self-centring systems, replaceable fuses and post-earthquake functional recovery
